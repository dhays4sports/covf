# Home Assessment Question Validity and Bias Audit

## Audit scope

ASMT-1.2 reviews the Home assessment at the question and answer level. The audit asks whether each prompt measures a clear construct, whether every response can reasonably support its assigned finding type, and whether the wording avoids implying that CoverageFit has determined policy adequacy.

The assessment remains an educational review-readiness tool. It does not read the issued policy, calculate replacement cost, determine appropriate limits, or recommend a carrier product automatically.

## Validity rules

Every active Home question must follow these rules:

1. **Ask about a verifiable state.** Prefer review recency, confirmed policy terms, deliberate decisions, or practical financial readiness over unanchored confidence.
2. **Separate knowledge from condition.** “I do not know” is uncertainty. A homeowner-confirmed missing or financially unworkable condition may be an identified gap. A known decision that still needs contextual evaluation is a consideration.
3. **Do not declare adequacy from one number.** No liability limit, deductible, or product choice automatically proves that protection is appropriate for every household.
4. **Do not penalize a deliberate decision as ignorance.** A homeowner who reviewed umbrella exposure and intentionally declined the product demonstrates more readiness than someone who never evaluated it.
5. **Require stronger evidence for a strength.** A positive answer must describe a confirmed or reviewed state, not only “I believe” or “I am confident.”
6. **Avoid duplicate answer meanings.** Each response must represent a distinct state and lead to a distinct or deliberately equivalent finding.
7. **Use neutral education.** Help text explains why the topic matters without steering the homeowner toward a predetermined product, limit, or outcome.
8. **Keep findings within the homeowner’s evidence.** Insights may restate the response and identify a review question, but may not infer exclusions, inadequacy, or missing coverage that the homeowner did not report.

## Question-by-question audit

| Topic | Construct | Prior validity issue | ASMT-1.2 resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dwelling rebuilding estimate | Review recency | Relied on self-rated confidence and treated confidence as evidence of a current reconstruction estimate | Asks when and how the estimate was reviewed; separates outdated review, known amount without methodology, and complete uncertainty |
| Additional rebuilding protection | Policy-term verification | A generic “Yes, I believe” earned a strength and confirmed absence was automatically treated as a full gap | Strength now requires knowing the amount or percentage; known absence is a neutral consideration; uncertainty remains distinct |
| Building-code upgrades | Policy-term verification | Confidence wording did not establish whether the term or limit was known | Requires confirmed amount, believed-but-unconfirmed inclusion, reported absence, or uncertainty |
| Water-loss terms | Policy-term verification | Broad confidence wording grouped several materially different water provisions together | Distinguishes reviewed terms, partial understanding, possible separate deductible or requirement, and no review |
| Deductible readiness | Financial readiness | Did not offer a path for homeowners who do not know the deductible and mixed affordability with policy knowledge | Separates known and fundable, unknown separate deductibles, financial strain, inability to fund, and unknown amounts |
| Personal liability | Exposure review | Treated `$500,000 or higher` as automatically strong and `$100,000 or less` as automatically inadequate | Evaluates whether the limit was reviewed against household exposure; a lower stated limit is a consideration, not an adequacy verdict |
| Belongings and valuable items | Policy-term verification | Confidence did not establish settlement method or special-limit review | Separates settlement-method knowledge from valuable-item review |
| Temporary living expenses | Policy-term verification | “No” and “Not sure” represented essentially the same uncertainty | Distinguishes known and reviewed fit, known amount without fit review, included-but-unknown amount, and complete uncertainty |
| Umbrella liability | Exposure review | Penalized not carrying an umbrella even when the decision may have been deliberate | A reviewed decision to carry or not carry is a strength; lack of review is a consideration; lack of understanding is uncertainty |
| Household and property changes | Change alignment | Did not provide a path for homeowners who could not identify their last full review | Adds an explicit review-history uncertainty response while preserving unreviewed reported changes as an identified gap |
| Separate hazards | Exposure review | The assessment did not ask whether commonly excluded or separately insured hazards had been evaluated | Adds a neutral earthquake, flood, and other separately handled hazard review without claiming a particular product is required |

## Finding validity contract

### Strength

A strength requires a homeowner-reported confirmation, review, or deliberate decision. It does not mean CoverageFit verified the policy or determined the protection is sufficient.

### Consideration

A consideration identifies a known state that needs household context. Examples include an older reconstruction review, a known absence of additional rebuilding protection, a liability limit not reviewed against current exposures, or an umbrella need that was never evaluated.

### Uncertainty

Uncertainty means the homeowner cannot verify the term, amount, review history, or product purpose. It must not be rewritten as a confirmed coverage gap.

### Identified gap

An identified gap requires an affirmative condition with a direct review consequence, such as an unfundable deductible, reported absence of building-code upgrade protection, or household or property changes that were not reviewed.

## Missing-domain decision

ASMT-1.2 adds **Separate Hazards** because a standard Home review should establish whether exclusions or separately insured causes of loss have been considered. The wording remains location-neutral and names earthquake and flood only as examples.

ASMT-1.3 now conditionally adds pool liability, detached-structure, and older-roof term questions only when the homeowner confirms the applicable characteristic. Confirmed older-home details may prioritize code-term verification. Home sharing, rental use, home-based business, and other exposures remain deferred because the current property profile does not collect them.

## Remaining limitations

- Answers remain self-reported and are not policy verification.
- Review recency thresholds are educational calibration choices, not carrier or regulatory standards.
- The revised questions improve construct validity but still require comprehension testing with real homeowners.
- Question and answer performance should be reviewed after a controlled pilot for abandonment, misunderstanding, and consultation usefulness.
